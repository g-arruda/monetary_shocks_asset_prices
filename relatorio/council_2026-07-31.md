### Referee recommendation: **Major Revision**

Not Reject. The harsh referee's Reject is calibrated to a submitted manuscript, and this is a draft whose abstract, intro and conclusion are unwritten and whose entire Robustez section is drafted-but-commented. Two of the three fatal-sounding charges — no VAR benchmark, no AR sets — are *missing work that the repository is already tooled to produce* (`script/model_var.R` exists; `mosw_strength_grid.csv` already has the full grid), not structural defects. But it is not R&R either: the identification concern is real, converged on independently by all three critics from three different directions, and it goes to whether the object being measured is a monetary policy shock at all.

### Identification verdict: **not established — the instrument plausibly selects sovereign-risk surprises in, and the paper has not tested otherwise**

All three critics arrived here separately. The harsh referee reads the response constellation (long-end pass-through ×1.85, BRL −3.64%, CDS/EMBI widening, positive core inflation) as a sovereign risk-premium signature. The macro theorist makes the sharper logical point: the JK sign filter screens *out* the benign confound (central-bank information effect: rates↑, stocks↑) but a domestic fiscal/sovereign surprise has rates↑, stocks↓, FX↑ — **exactly the pattern the filter retains as "policy."** The methodologist arrives from the data: Gonçalves et al. (2025) find appreciation and no CDS response on Brazilian high-frequency data, the opposite sign.

Three things follow. First, the placebo battery cannot discharge this — a domestic fiscal shock also should not move the S&P 500, so passing placebos is uninformative against this specific alternative. Second, the paper's own commented-out state-dependence result *cuts against* the fiscal-dominance reading it wants: the impact FX response is not state-dependent in sovereign risk under any of seven indicators (|t| ≤ 1.14). The paper currently asserts the regime story and suppresses the evidence against it. Third, this is also the paper's biggest opportunity — the macro theorist is right that the Gonçalves disagreement, written up as a genuine puzzle rather than rationalized in a footnote, is more interesting than the replication.

### Top 3 required revisions

**1. Test the sovereign-risk confound directly, then either discharge it or reframe the paper around it.** Cheap and decisive: regress same-day CDS/EMBI changes on the instrument over the ~62 retained Copom days. A significant loading means the surprise is contaminated at daily frequency, before any DFM. Add a narrative audit of retained days against fiscal/political news, and consider a three-way JK classification with a sovereign-risk category. If the confound survives, the honest paper is "what a Copom-day surprise identifies in a fiscal-dominance economy," which is a better paper than the replication.

**2. Estimate the VAR benchmark. The paper's stated contribution is currently untested.** Two critics flagged this independently. Alessi-Kerssenfischer's entire claim is DFM-vs-small-VAR; this draft asserts "stronger and faster than small models find" and never runs the small model, while its headline asset block has zero sig90 cells. `script/model_var.R` exists and shares the identification module. Until it is in the text, the title claim is unsupported — and the equity result points the other way.

**3. Kill the (r,q) post-selection problem by showing the plateau, and build the AR sets.** Both corroborated. The paper selected (7,6) by maximizing ξ_mp over four cells and shows only those four — but `mosw_strength_grid.csv` shows (7,5), (7,7), (8,5), (8,6) also clear 10 in both windows. Printing the full 14-cell grid plus IRF overlays at 3-4 cells converts "specification hunting" into "documented plateau" at near-zero cost. Separately: ξ_mp = 10.43 is a heuristic, 24/147 leave-one-out drops fall below it, and §3.6 names AR sets as the correct remedy without constructing them — as compiled, the paper implies the problem is solved. Even one AR interval at h=0 for the key series would close this.

### Top 3 optional revisions

**1. Uncomment Robustez, or at minimum repair the dangling active claims.** Line 365 currently states the BCB metals index rises 3.43% (sig90 through h=4) with the discharging clause — that the USD-denominated version does not respond — commented out on the same line. As compiled this reads as an unaddressed exogeneity failure on the paper's own placebo logic. Same for the `fig:acoes` note promising a discussion "no texto" that is commented out, and the `fig:cambio` regime sentence at 369.

**2. Fix the structural-citation layer.** Three targeted edits: the financial accelerator is Bernanke-Gertler-Gilchrist (1999), not Gertler-Karadi (2015); Cooley-Quadrini's heterogeneity margin is firm net worth, not sector, and the sectoral credit result conflates it with earmarked-vs-free credit (Plano Safra), which BCB data can actually split; and the cost channel (Barth-Ramey, Ravenna-Walsh) jointly predicts the positive short-horizon price response *and* working-capital credit expansion, unifying two currently-separate post-hoc readings with a citation whose heterogeneity dimension actually matches. Also reconcile line 218 (endorsing Bauer-Swanson's no-information-effect claim) with line 280 (narrating retained days in exactly the information-effect language BS rejects).

**3. Document the bootstrap mechanics — including the two things that narrow the bands.** The paper should *state* the two things the code gets right and most papers get wrong (same Rademacher draw for instrument and residuals; factor model re-estimated inside each replication). It should also report the failed-draw count out of 800, since failures are replaced by the point estimate and mechanically narrow every band, and note that `Idio` is fixed across draws, which understates Λ̂ sampling variability. The Kilian asymmetry (bias-corrected DGP, OLS point estimate) is faithful to `DFMest_BLL.m` and is defensible — but it needs one sentence saying so, or readers will read it as a bug, as one critic did.

### Where the critics disagree — report, don't resolve

**The joint Wald paragraph.** The harsh referee says the paper reads it backwards: low joint-F (2.33/2.70) is presented as *evidence of exogeneity*, but under relevance-plus-exogeneity a shock loading on multiple factor innovations should give a *large* joint Wald; a joint Wald of 13.99 on 6 df (p≈0.03) is weak evidence of relevance, not proof of exogeneity. The macro theorist praises the same passage as a methodological contribution. **The synthesis sides with the harsh referee on the econometrics** — the statistic does not have the interpretation the paragraph assigns it — while noting the theorist's point survives in weaker form: choosing (r,q) on relevance in *both* sample windows rather than on an information criterion is a defensible and under-used practice. Rewrite the paragraph; keep the practice.

**The two-tier 68%/90% rule.** The methodologist calls it uncorrected multiple testing and "post-hoc softening"; the harsh referee calls it honestly applied and a strength. **Both are partly right, and there is a resolution neither reached.** The 706/2,597 figure is 27.2% — *below* the ~32% a pointwise 68% band would deliver under a pure null, which sounds damning. But the paper's own placebos hit 68% only 5 times in 147 (3.4%), far below nominal, which says the bootstrap bands are conservative, not that the signal is noise. **The right null benchmark is the empirical placebo rate, not the nominal level** — and against 3.4%, a 27% hit rate with a clear U-shaped horizon structure is meaningful. Make that argument explicitly; it converts the paper's weakest-looking number into a defended one. It does not, however, license pathwise claims ("the curve reverts between h=17 and h=33") from pointwise bands — that needs Montiel Olea-Plagborg-Møller sup-t bands, which is a genuine gap.

### What each critic caught alone

- **Harsh referee (code-verified, highest confidence):** the `juros_selic`/`juros_cdi` double-counting; the bootstrap failure fallback and fixed `Idio`; the equity-index band-width ratio (10.46 for the 8 cumulated-return indices vs 0.944 for level series at h=36 vs impact), which may *mechanically produce* the null equity result — the paper's own title subject. That last one is the single most underrated finding across all three reviews.
- **Macro theorist alone:** that the paper's own state-dependence evidence contradicts its fiscal-dominance claim; that the Castelnuovo-Nisticò citation is used for the wrong half of its result and would be load-bearing if repositioned as an identification argument; that the long-end amplification is inconsistent with the expectations hypothesis for a transitory shock, and the untested alternative — that the identified innovation is closer to a curve-level shift — would rescale every number in the paper.
- **Methodologist alone:** no unit-root/cointegration diagnostics anywhere; no uniform bands for pathwise claims; the BS first stage (R² = 0.024) reported as a number with no coefficients, SEs or residual diagnostics shown.

### Critic 1 — Harsh Referee (Top-5 Macro), Claude Opus

**Referee Recommendation**

Reject — the paper's stated contribution (that a large-information DFM delivers stronger asset-price responses than small-dimensional models) is never actually estimated, its headline asset-price block is null at conventional significance, and the identified shock has a constellation of responses that is more consistent with a sovereign-risk shock than a monetary policy shock — a confound that the paper's own Jarociński-Karadi filter systematically *selects in* rather than out in a fiscal-dominance economy.

**Summary Assessment**

The paper estimates a non-stationary dynamic factor model on ~106 Brazilian monthly series (2013:01-2025:09), identifies a monetary policy shock via a Copom-day DI-futures surprise purified à la Bauer-Swanson and sign-filtered à la Jarociński-Karadi, and traces responses of yields, FX, sovereign risk, activity, credit, prices, and equities. The execution has real quality: the MOSW weak-instrument diagnostic is used correctly as the relevance ruler instead of a first-stage F, the wild bootstrap correctly multiplies the instrument by the same Rademacher draw (`impulse_responde.R:636-637`) and re-estimates the factor model inside each draw, and the numerical claims in the prose reconcile exactly with the stored IRF output that I checked (yield_3m 27.9bp, yield_6m 0.005, Selic trough −77.8bp at h=34).

The single biggest problem is identification. A 50bp surprise at the 6-month DI produces: +91.6bp at 2y and +92.7bp at 5y (i.e. long-end pass-through of ~1.85×, against +27.9bp at 3m), a 3.64% *depreciation* of the real, +29.1bp on the 5y CDS, +20.0bp on the EMBI, +0.88pp on monthly producer inflation, and a core-EX0 response that is positive at *every* horizon from h=12 to h=48. That is not the signature of a monetary policy shock under any standard model; it is the textbook signature of a sovereign risk-premium/fiscal shock. And the JK filter cannot separate the two here: JK's rule keeps days where rates rise and equities fall, which in the US isolates policy from the CB-information confound — but in Brazil the dominant confound (fiscal/sovereign risk news) has *exactly the same sign pattern* as policy. The filter discards the benign confounder and retains the malign one. The paper gestures at this ("dominância fiscal") but treats it as an interpretation rather than as a threat requiring a discriminating test.

**Required Revisions**

1. The Jarociński-Karadi sign filter is not identifying in a fiscal-dominance economy — provide a discriminating test. Report the Wed→Thu change in the 5y CDS and the EMBI on the 62 retained days and regress it on the instrument; implement a three-way classification (policy / sovereign risk / information); provide a narrative audit of the 62 retained days against major fiscal/political announcements.
2. The paper's stated contribution is not delivered: there is no VAR benchmark. `script/model_var.R` exists in the repository — estimate it on the same factors, same instrument, same normalization, and report the two IRF sets overlaid.
3. Long-horizon IRFs from a levels VAR on non-stationary factors — the entire medium-run tier is on unsound inferential footing. BLL standardization fixes PCA scaling, not VAR stationarity. Estimate the cointegration rank among the 7 factors; report companion-matrix eigenvalue moduli to rule out a dominant complex eigenvalue pair (~60mo period) mimicking economics.
4. Cross-variable agreement is mechanically imposed by the DFM and cannot be used as corroborating evidence — every response is Λᵢ times the same 7-dim factor IRF. `juros_selic` and `juros_cdi` are numerically near-identical at every horizon checked, so passages citing "both" as independent evidence double-count one series.
5. (r,q) is selected on first-stage strength, and Table 1 shows a selected subset of the grid — `mosw_strength_grid.csv` shows (7,5),(7,7),(8,5),(8,6) also clear 10 in both windows. Report the full grid and show IRFs at multiple (r,q) side by side.
6. Compute the Anderson-Rubin sets, and disclose the fragility of ξ_mp — named as the remedy in §3.6 but never constructed; the disclosure of this gap is itself commented out.
7. The interpretation of the joint Wald statistic is econometrically wrong: low joint-F is read as evidence of exogeneity, but under relevance+exogeneity a policy shock loading on multiple factor innovations should produce a *large* joint Wald. Delete or correct this paragraph.
8. Equity indices enter as monthly returns (cumulated) while 98 other series enter in levels/logs — inconsistent with BLL and Alessi-Kerssenfischer; own commented diagnostic shows band-width ratio at h=36 vs impact is 10.46 for the 8 indices vs 0.944 for level series. Re-estimate with B3 indices in log level.
9. The compiled draft has no robustness section, and active claims now dangle: line 365 reports the BCB metals index rising 3.43% sig90 through h=4 with the defending sentence commented out on the same line — reads as an unaddressed exogeneity failure as compiled.

**Important but Not Fatal:** Lag order p=6 asserted without IC evidence (~3.3 obs/parameter); bootstrap point estimate uses plain OLS while DGP uses Kilian-corrected coefficients with naive percentile CIs — possible mis-centering; `Idio` held fixed across bootstrap draws and failed draws silently replaced by point estimate; yield curve is externally-supplied non-reproducible data setting the scale of every IRF; heavy within-block panel redundancy (8 EPU series, 5 regional employment series, etc.); PIB acumulado no ano is a YTD series with January reset entered as log-level without transformation; structural stories (credit-line drawdown, financial accelerator) have untested auxiliary implications; the Gonçalves et al. sign disagreement may be the paper's most interesting result but its candidate explanations are commented out.

**Minor Comments:** "~110 séries" vs 106 in appendix; 95 of ~102 scheduled Copom meetings; report effective non-zero-instrument-month count; financial accelerator misattributed to Gertler-Karadi (2015) instead of Bernanke-Gertler-Gilchrist (1999); uncited comparative claim about Brazilian event-study magnitudes; AR intervals invoked in §3.6 with zero prior introduction since the threshold discussion is commented out; zero results tables anywhere; abstract and Conclusão need to be written.

**Strengths Worth Preserving:** MOSW ξ diagnostic used correctly and validated against published Kilian-oil numbers; bootstrap correctly re-draws the same Rademacher multiplier and re-estimates the factor model inside each replication — neither currently stated in the paper; the two-tier 68%/90% reporting rule is applied consistently and honestly; the paper reports several results against its own preferred narrative (IBC-Br not following sectoral trough, wrong-signed employment at impact, EX0 core flagged as the one "incoherent" verdict, null equity block stated flatly, price puzzle surviving the JK filter meant to kill it).

### Critic 2 — Macro Theorist (DSGE/HANK), Claude Opus

*Adapted brief: the paper has no structural model, so this critic assessed discipline in the paper's use of structural-theory citations as motivation/interpretation.*

**Theory Verdict:** Needs revision — but only in the structural-citation layer. The paper is honestly and correctly framed as a reduced-form dynamic-causal-effects exercise and never claims a structural decomposition, welfare statement, or GE channel accounting; the problems are that three of its economic interpretations (Cooley–Quadrini for sectoral credit, the "financial accelerator" for spreads, fiscal dominance for the FX/CDS signature) are labels attached to correlations by mappings too loose to be falsified, and one theoretical over-promise survives in the Intro that the empirical section cannot deliver.

**Structural-Citation Issues**

1. Cooley–Quadrini is mis-mapped: their heterogeneity dimension is firm net worth/size/financing margin, not "sector." Sectoral credit aggregates conflate this with an orthogonal margin (earmarked vs. free credit in Brazil, e.g. Plano Safra). The paper offers two explanations with no way to distinguish them observationally; a naive CQ reading predicts differential contraction, not the observed expansion. Falsifiable test available: split by recursos livres/direcionados or firm size.
2. "Acelerador financeiro de Gertler-Karadi (2015)" is a mis-attribution — GK15 measures credit-cost responses; the mechanism is Bernanke-Gertler-Gilchrist (1999). The observed pattern (compression at impact, widening only h=11-14) is explained by slow stock-repricing, which is also the null hypothesis with zero accelerator content. A real discriminating test is available in the paper's own panel (cross-sectional correlation of spread widening with equity/net-worth response).
3. Fiscal dominance is asserted as "a regime's prediction" with no model, test, or citation to FTPL/sovereign-premium literature. Most serious: the paper's own commented-out state-dependence evidence contradicts the strongest observable implication — impact FX response is not state-dependent in sovereign risk under any of 7 indicators (|t| ≤ 1.14). The JK filter cannot screen a domestic sovereign-risk surprise (rates↑, stocks↓, FX↑, CDS↑ passes as "policy") — observationally identical to the claimed fiscal-dominance signature. Placebos cannot discharge this since a domestic fiscal shock also shouldn't move the S&P 500.
4. The paper endorses two mutually exclusive theories of the same phenomenon: line 218 adopts Bauer-Swanson's claim that no CB information effect is needed to generate the pattern; line 280 then narrates the JK-filtered days using exactly the "central bank revealed favorable information" language BS just rejected.
5. Long-end amplification (+92.7bp at 5y from a 6m shock) is inconsistent with the expectations hypothesis for a transitory shock. The paper only entertains the risk-premium reading, not the alternative that the normalization itself understates the shock (the identified innovation may be closer to a curve-level shift, rescaling every number in the paper).
6. "Coerência forte/incoerência" verdicts are reported without showing the reader the underlying theoretical windows, sources, or tier definitions — unfalsifiable as presented.
7. Missed unifying mechanism: the "cost channel" (Barth-Ramey 2001, Ravenna-Walsh 2006) jointly predicts positive short-horizon prices and impact credit expansion concentrated in working-capital-intensive sectors — a citation whose heterogeneity dimension actually matches, unlike Cooley-Quadrini.
8. The "financial channel" story for equities (IMOB/SMLL falling more than the broad index) is equally consistent with a duration/discount-rate channel; the discriminating evidence (impact response correlates +0.903 with each index's measured 2y-DI sensitivity) is commented out.

**GE / PE Assessment:** Disciplined overall — never claims GE decomposition or touches welfare. Line 256 ("recupera choques fundamentais") over-claims relative to line 210's correct hedge, and the two passages currently contradict each other. The commented line 363's "propagação de equilíbrio geral" phrasing should be reworded (not deleted) before restoration to avoid implying an equilibrium-feedback decomposition that isn't actually done.

**Expectations Set vs. Delivered**

1. The Intro promises a "channel decomposition" the design cannot produce — a proxy-SVAR/DFM IRF measures the response *of* asset prices, not a decomposition of the output response into channels. The Castelnuovo citation specifically primes a stock-wealth-effect-on-consumption expectation never tested (and can't be, given the null equity block).
2. The paper's own stated main contribution ("stronger and faster than small models find") is never tested — no small-VAR benchmark exists in the `.tex` despite `script/model_var.R` existing, and the equity result (null) is the opposite of "stronger than thought."
3. Castelnuovo-Nisticò is cited for the wrong half of its result — its headline (the estimated policy rule responds to stock prices) is actually an identification argument for what the BS orthogonalization layer must purge, not just motivating color.
4. The BCB metals commodity index result reads as an unaddressed exogeneity failure in the compiled draft because the discharging sentence is commented out along with all of `sec:exogeneidade`.

**Strengths:** Honest about being reduced-form where it matters; the two-tier 90%/68% rule is stated and respected; the Gertler-Karadi policy-instrument-vs-policy-indicator distinction is used correctly and sharpened with real Brazilian institutional detail; the fiscal-dominance reading, though under-argued, has three independently co-moving observable implications all measured at 90% and needs to be written down as a joint signature; the Stock-Watson SVAR-IV/LP-IV taxonomy passage (lines 206-210) is careful and correct; the relevance-selection discipline (choosing r,q on ξ_mp in both sample windows) is a real methodological contribution; the instrument construction genuinely stacks BS and JK rather than just claiming one and doing the other.
